Background

The Miyawaki Forest within the campus of the Indian Institute of Technology Palakkad was established in 2020. The selected site, covering about 1600 m², was previously dominated by the invasive species Gliricidia sepium. To prepare the area, all Gliricidia vegetation was carefully removed, and the land was divided into four equal quadrants for plantation, with pathways reserved for walking access. During ground preparation, the topsoil was excavated to a depth of one metre. Half of the excavated soil was refilled into the pit and evenly distributed. A nutrient-rich soil medium—comprising farmyard manure, cocopeat, and rice straw in a 1:1:1 ratio—was then added at a rate of approximately 100 kg per square metre. The remaining native soil was used to fill and level the surface. This method keeps the soil loose, aerated, and highly conducive to uniform plant growth. Tree species suitable for local conditions were identified with guidance from the Kerala Forest Research Institute (KFRI). Predominantly native trees with fruit-bearing, flowering, timber, or medicinal value were selected (the complete species list is available in the Data section). Planting density ranged from 2 to 7 saplings per square metre, amounting to roughly 4800 plants in total. Each sapling was treated with Vesicular Arbuscular Mycorrhiza (VAM) at 30 g per plant to promote healthy root growth. VAM is a symbiotic fungus that enhances the tree’s ability to absorb nutrients and minerals from the soil while receiving carbohydrates in return. Regular watering was carried out every two days for the first six months. Thereafter, irrigation frequency was reduced to once every seven days during summer and once a month during winter over the following three years. Dead saplings were replaced whenever possible in the early stages. Occasionally, the site experiences visits by wild elephants, leading to minor damage to some trees. Despite these challenges, the forest has shown steady and resilient growth, contributing to improved local biodiversity and microclimatic regulation within the campus. After three years, the site has become self-sustaining, requiring no managerial intervention, and now thrives as a naturally balanced ecosystem supporting local biodiversity.

Research

Current research at the Miyawaki Forest site focuses on understanding the ecological functioning and carbon dynamics of the system. Key areas of investigation include:

  • Quantification of Carbon Sequestration Rates
  • Photosynthetic Traits and Modelling

Teaching

The Miyawaki Forest site serves as a living laboratory for project activities under the course Ecology and Environment (ES 1010), taught to first-year B.Tech. students at IIT Palakkad. As part of the course, students work in groups to measure tree allometric parameters—such as height, diameter, etc for individual trees within the site. These measurements are then used in allometric equations to estimate tree biomass and carbon storage. The data collected through these exercises not only enhances students’ understanding of ecological and environmental processes, but also contributes to a long-term dataset aimed at monitoring the growth dynamics of individual tree species and the overall functioning of the ecosystem.
